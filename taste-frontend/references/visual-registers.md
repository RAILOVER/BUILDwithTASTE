# Visual registers

The taste skills this one sits on top of lean toward a single register by default: refined, editorial, premium-minimalist, the Linear and Stripe family. That is the right answer for many briefs. It is still a default, and reaching for it without considering the alternative produces work that is competent and anonymous, which is the exact failure this skill exists to prevent.

Register selection is an explicit decision, made and named during brand strategy, not something that falls out of token choices later.

---

## Register A: premium-minimalist (the default)

Refined type, generous air, restrained palette, motion that is felt rather than noticed. The corpus references closest to it are agrumeafarm and a24.raviklaassens: warm or neutral grounds, licensed display faces, imagery doing the heavy lifting. Most client work lands here.

---

## Register B: technical / generative

Reference point in the corpus: **dithergarden.com**. What defines it:

- **Monospace as the voice, not as a code style.** Body copy, headings, labels, all of it. JetBrains Mono, IBM Plex Mono, Berkeley Mono.
- **Near-monochrome chrome.** The color budget is spent on the product's actual output, the dithered image, the glitch, the depth map, never on decorative interface. The interface gets out of the way of the thing it produces.
- **The mechanism is the content.** Dither Garden names all fifteen of its algorithms in the interface: Floyd-Steinberg, Atkinson, Stucki, Bayer, Blue-Noise, Riemersma. Process transparency is the design position, not a FAQ entry hidden at the bottom.
- **The artifact is the aesthetic.** Noise, dithering, compression glitch, grain. Things a premium-minimalist page would treat as defects to remove become the subject.
- **Utilitarian density.** Directness reads as credibility here, where whitespace-as-luxury would read as marketing. Pairs naturally with `industrial-brutalist-ui` if that skill is installed.

**This only works when the artifact is real**, produced by the product doing its job. Decorative noise with no functional source is the same failure as a gradient blob: texture standing in for an idea.

### When it fits

- The product's core value is a visible technical or generative process, image or video processing, data visualisation, procedural generation, AI tooling whose output is interesting on its own
- The audience is technical or creative-technical, people who read raw as authentic rather than unfinished
- The brand's tone words point to direct, experimental, unpretentious rather than polished or reassuring

### When it does not

- Enterprise procurement buyers, where raw reads as under-resourced
- Broad consumer audiences, where monospace everywhere reads as niche
- Products whose output is not visually interesting on its own. This register has nothing to hide behind; premium-minimalist can carry a plainer product competently.

---

## Choosing

Do not drift into Register B because it is more fun to build. It is a deliberate offer, not a house style. When the product and audience fit the criteria above, present it during brand strategy as a real option alongside premium-minimalist, using the corpus references as concrete examples, and let that conversation decide. Record the choice and the reasoning so the token work downstream derives from the right direction.
