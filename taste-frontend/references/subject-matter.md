# Subject matter

A measured audit of the client's reference corpus against work this account actually shipped produced one finding that outweighed every other combined:

**The reference sites are frames around beautiful subject matter. Losing work is a beautiful frame around nothing.**

Agrumea is gorgeous because it is full of photographs of Sicilian citrus preserves. A24 is gorgeous because it renders real film artwork. noireternel is gorgeous because it is 588 frames of a perfume bottle turning in light. Paxter carries 136 images and 16 videos of actual work. Don't Look Up carries 58.

The rejected build contained two generated line drawings and some CSS, and several rounds were spent refining motion, spacing and easing while the page remained empty of anything worth looking at. No amount of frame polish substitutes for substance. This file exists so that never happens again.

---

## The subject audit, at intake

Before any design work, answer in writing:

**What does this brand have that is worth looking at?**

Then classify it:

| Answer | What the page is built around |
|---|---|
| A physical product | Photography or rendered footage of it, generously sized. This is the Agrumea and noireternel model. |
| A body of work | The work itself, presented as a catalogue with real metadata. The A24 model. |
| A visible process | The process running, output as imagery. The Dither Garden model. |
| A place, people, craft | Documentary photography. |
| **Nothing photogenic** | **Stop. This is the hard case.** |

### When the honest answer is nothing

A B2B dashboard has no jars, no films, no bottle. This is the common case for software, and pretending otherwise is how a build ends up as motion polish on an empty page.

It is also a solved problem. **heronaiapp.com is the proof**: an AI compliance checker for architects, no physical object anywhere, no 3D, no video, and it holds its own against the rest of the corpus. Study that entry before reaching for anything exotic.

Four routes, in order of preference:

1. **Make the product's output the imagery.** Heron renders real code-violation callouts over architectural drawings (`IBC 1015.3 GUARDRAIL REQUIRED FOR FALL PROTECTION`). Not a screenshot of the interface, the *work the product does*, treated as the subject and art-directed like photography. This is almost always the right answer for software.
2. **Make one moment playable.** Heron's hero says `CLICK AND DRAG YOUR MOUSE TO SEE VIOLATIONS` and lets you do the product's core action before reading a word. Interaction is subject matter: it is the thing the corpus has that a passive scroll does not, and it costs no photography at all.
3. **Find the human subject around the product.** Not the interface, the week it describes: the desk at dawn, the empty room, the light at the end of the day. Works well and generates cleanly, but it is atmosphere rather than evidence, so it cannot carry a page alone.
4. **Say so to the client.** If nothing above applies and the budget will not cover creating imagery, that constrains what the site can be, and naming it early beats discovering it at review.

Note what is *not* on this list: commissioning 3D objects to stand in for a missing product. Rendered abstract shapes are the most common way software sites try to solve this, and none of the corpus references do it. Heron gets there with 45 flat images and one interaction.

Record the answer alongside the brand strategy. It determines the entire shape of the build.

## Asset floor

A page whose only imagery is line art is not finished. Minimums before a build counts as complete:

| Build | Floor |
|---|---|
| Landing or brand page | **8 distinct real assets minimum**, 12 or more preferred |
| Catalogue or portfolio | One real asset per item, no placeholders |
| Cinematic set piece | The set-piece media, plus 6 or more supporting assets elsewhere |
| Single-screen or cold open | 3 minimum, each substantial |

"Distinct" means different subject matter. The same mark repeated seven times is one asset, which is exactly what the rejected build had.

Count them before pre-flight. If the count is under the floor, the fix is generating more, not spacing out what exists.

---

## Density beats polish

Sequence matters. Fill the page with subject first, then refine.

The rejected build inverted this: several rounds tuning easing curves, logo sizing and alignment on a page that had nothing in it. Those rounds were not wasted exactly, the details did improve, but they could not move the outcome, because the missing thing was never the easing.

**When a page feels flat, check the asset count before touching the motion.**

---

## Scale

The corpus is unanimous: full bleed, edge to edge, viewport as canvas. Imagery runs to the edges; type is set large; one idea holds a screen.

A boxed layout at 1040px reads as a slide deck no matter how well it is composed. Reserve contained widths for running body copy, and even then let the imagery break out.
